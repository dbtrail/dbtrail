- **PostgreSQL port: a division and an `avg()` written without `round()` are answered by the copy** (#3219). A statement that divided a `numeric` (`sum(a) / sum(b)`, `100.0 * x / y`, `amount / 100`) or took a bare `avg()` of a `numeric` or of whole numbers went to the source, because PostgreSQL writes that quotient with a number of decimals that changes with the value. Percentages, shares and averages are most of what a report computes, so most reports ran on the source. The copy now answers them: the exact quotient rounded to 16 decimals, a `numeric` as PostgreSQL's is. PostgreSQL's number of decimals depends on the value and on both sides: often 16 for a quotient from 1 to 9999, the same text (`7.5000000000000000`), 12 for a larger one, 20 for a smaller one (`0.3333333333333333` for `0.33333333333333333333`). Where such a value filters, groups, sorts or is summed, the answer can differ when it turns on a digit past the sixteenth decimal: `a / b * b = a` can be true on one side and false on the other, and a `GROUP BY a / b` can split a group. `round(a / b, k)` and `round(avg(x), k)` are unchanged and give PostgreSQL's text exactly. The copy answers when the statement itself shows each side to be a `numeric` or a whole number: numbers, columns of whole numbers or of a `numeric` of at most 20 digits and 8 decimals, `+`, `-`, `*`, and `count`, `sum`, `min`, `max`, `abs`, `coalesce`, `nullif`, `greatest`, `least` and `length` over them. Everything else goes to the source as before: a divisor of zero (PostgreSQL's error), two whole numbers (PostgreSQL divides them as whole numbers), a `double precision` on a side, a name from a subquery, a quotient under 0.000001 that is not zero. A server without a forwarding user divides and averages as before. MySQL and MariaDB sources are not affected.
