### Added

- PostgreSQL port: `round(a / b, k)` and `round(avg(x), k)` (also with `avg` times 10, 100 or any other power of ten) are answered on the copy, rounded as PostgreSQL rounds a `numeric`: the exact quotient, half away from zero, to exactly `k` decimals (`50.00`, not `50.0`). Through a forwarding account they used to go to the source; three of the fourteen reports of a TPC-C analytics workload now stay on the copy. Checked against PostgreSQL 16 by `internal/pgcompare` over 1,780 random and exactly-half quotients. What the copy cannot round the same way it refuses, and the statement goes to the source: a division of two whole numbers (PostgreSQL divides those as whole numbers), a float, a quotient that lands so close to a half that PostgreSQL's own rounding of the division could reach it, and a number past 128 bits. Without a forwarding account the copy now rounds these the way PostgreSQL does too, and answers the rare near-half case with an error that says why.
