- **One slow answer from the index no longer costs the capture lock** (#3036, part of #2513). The process that captures a source checks its lock on the index with a question, and gave that question 5 seconds: on a loaded host one late answer was taken for a lost lock, and capture restarted and replayed from its last saved position (measured: six times in 75 minutes). The timeout was itself what freed the lock, because cancelling a statement closes its connection and MySQL frees a session's locks with it. The question now waits for its answer and the lock is checked every second instead of every 5. The holder gives the lock up 10 seconds after it sent the last check the index answered, the bound it already had, so a process that takes over still never writes beside the previous one. One slow answer among prompt ones can take up to about 9 seconds, with a warning in the log past 5; an index that takes more than about 5 seconds for every answer still loses the lock, because two answers in a row have to fit in the 10 seconds. A dead connection or a lock held by another session stops capture at the next check, as before.
