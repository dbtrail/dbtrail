- **PostgreSQL port: `substr()` is answered by the copy, and a cut of a `char(n)` column too** (#3219). `substr(x, n)` and `substr(x, n, m)` always went to the source, though `substring` with the same arguments was answered by the copy. They now follow `substring`'s rule: the copy answers when the start and the length are positive whole numbers written in the statement. Under that rule `substr` and `substring` of a `char(n)` column stay on the copy too, which keeps the column's padding: where the cut reaches the padding the copy answers `b  ` and PostgreSQL `b`, and a comparison of that cut (`substr(code, 1, 3) = 'b'`) can miss the row. A cut inside the part with data is the same on both. A `char(n)` column compared, joined, sorted or grouped on its own still goes to the source. MySQL and MariaDB sources are not affected.
