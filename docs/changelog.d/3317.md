- **The copy adds the filter an `OR` implies** (#3317). When a statement's `WHERE`, or the `ON` of an inner join, holds an `OR` whose every branch says `col = literal` or `col IN (literals)` among its conditions, the copy runs it with `col IN (every one of those literals)` added in `AND`, so the table is filtered as it is read. The filter is implied by the `OR`, so the answer does not change. CH-benCHmark Q19, which says `ol_w_id IN (...)` in each of its three branches, went from 1.07 s to 0.42 s on a copy of 100 warehouses; Q7 gets the filter on `n1.n_name` and `n2.n_name`. It applies to the PostgreSQL port and to the MySQL port with read routing, only to the text the copy runs, after the statement was routed: the source, the client and the audit record keep the statement as written. Left as it is: a branch without that column, the column written two ways (`ol_w_id` and `order_line.ol_w_id`), placeholders, `NULL`, subqueries, casts or expressions in place of a literal, literals of two types, `NOT IN`, `<>`, `IS NULL`, an `OR` under `NOT`, in the select list, a `CASE`, `HAVING`, or the `ON` of an outer join, and statements that call `random()`. `IN` compares text as `=` does on the copy (case folded for MySQL, a `_bin` column and PostgreSQL by their bytes, a trailing space counted). If a step of the rewrite fails, the statement runs as it is and a warning is logged.
