- **PostgreSQL port: four statements the copy answered otherwise than PostgreSQL now go to the source** (#3069, #3070, #3071, #3072). Each was measured against PostgreSQL 16, answered by the copy with no error, and is now sent to your PostgreSQL by the port's screen, with the forms the copy answers the same left on the copy:
  - **`substring` with a start of zero or below** (#3071): `substring('apple', -1, 3)` is `a` on PostgreSQL and was `e` through the port, which counted a negative start from the end. Only a start, and a length, written as whole numbers above zero stay on the copy (`substring(name, 1, 3)`, `substring(name FROM 2 FOR 3)`); a start read from a column or an expression goes to the source, since it may be negative.
  - **Date and time literals outside one form** (#3072): `'26-01-05'::date` is 2026-01-05 on PostgreSQL and was the year 26 through the port, `TIMESTAMP '2026-01-05 10:30:00.1234567'` is rounded to `.123457` there and was cut at `.123456`, and `TIME '24:00:00'` was midnight. A `DATE`, `TIMESTAMP` or `TIME` literal stays on the copy when it is written as `YYYY-MM-DD`, `HH:MM[:SS]` with at most six decimals and an hour below 24, or both; a string compared with a `date`, `timestamp` or `time` column is held to the same form.
  - **`CASE`, `COALESCE`, `GREATEST` and `LEAST` over numbers of different decimals** (#3070): PostgreSQL writes each value with its own decimals, the copy wrote them all with the most. `coalesce(amount, 0)` over a `numeric(12,2)` gave `0.00` for a NULL where PostgreSQL gives `0`, and `greatest(a, b)` of a `numeric(10,2)` and a `numeric(10,4)` gave `10.5000` for `10.50`. These go to the source now, with or without a `round()`. Written with the column's decimals (`coalesce(amount, 0.00)`) the statement stays on the copy, as do values that are all whole numbers and values that hold a `double precision` or a `real`.
  - **Arithmetic and `sum()` over a `real` column** (#3069): PostgreSQL computes a `real` as a `double precision`, so `r + 1` with `r = -0.1` is `0.8999999985098839` there and was `0.9` through the port, and `sum(r)` was `4.399999998509884` for `4.4`. Arithmetic over a `double precision`, a whole number or a `numeric` stays on the copy, and so does a `real` that is only read, compared, counted or taken the `min` or `max` of.

  What to expect: a statement of one of these forms that the copy used to answer is now answered by your PostgreSQL, with the right answer and at the source's speed. The most common one is `coalesce(amount, 0)` and `CASE ... ELSE 0 END` over a `numeric` column with decimals; writing the `0` as `0.00` keeps it on the copy. A connection without a forwarding user is not changed by this: there the copy still answers these statements as it did.
