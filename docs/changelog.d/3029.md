- **A PostgreSQL snapshot checks the free disk before it starts** (#3029). The first copy of a PostgreSQL server's tables, and every full read after it, was started without a look at the disk, where a MySQL full read has been checked since #1938. Before anything is read or created, DBTrail now sizes the tables the snapshot copies (`pg_table_size`: heap and TOAST, no indexes; a partitioned table by its partitions) and compares the sum with the free space of the server's Local folder, which is where a PostgreSQL snapshot is written. With less than 1 GiB free there, the room a snapshot update keeps free, the snapshot is refused with the numbers and recorded as a disk failure; nothing is read and no replication slot is created. With less than 1.3× the tables' size free it runs with a warning, which after a snapshot that fit reads as a note about the next one. The size of the tables alone never refuses: measured on PostgreSQL 16, a snapshot came to between 0.007× and 1.22× of it depending on the data (0.22× for ordinary order rows), so no fraction of it is a line every snapshot that fits stays above. Sizes that cannot be read, or a disk that cannot be measured, let the snapshot go ahead and say so. Tables on a storage other than the heap, whose reported size can be a compressed one, are counted and named, and the check then says it cannot tell instead of saying there is room. `docs/capacity.md` has the rule and the measurements.
