- **PostgreSQL: a table's delta chain ends at 12 pairs, so the copy no longer slows down for hours between rewrites** (#3290). With PostgreSQL table deltas on, each snapshot update adds a pair of small files beside a changed table, and every statement on the copy reads the table's file and all its pairs. The chain ended only when it was a day old, passed half of the table's file, or reached the retention line, which on a 6h retention meant about 51 pairs; reports ran 2 to 4 times slower just before a chain ended than just after. A chain now also ends at 12 pairs (an hour of updates five minutes apart), and the next update writes the table from its file and pairs. That costs a few minutes of work an hour for large tables (measured: 80 s, 246 s and 225 s for three tables of a 100-warehouse CH-benCHmark, on one update), and the first update after upgrading ends every long chain at once. MySQL and MariaDB are unchanged.
