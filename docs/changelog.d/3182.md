- **PostgreSQL router: a statement for a busy copy gets an error to retry, and is no longer sent to your PostgreSQL** (#3109). A statement headed for the copy that found every worker of the copy taken, and its `--max-wait` over or the line for a worker full, was sent to the source instead, over the simple and the extended protocol, heavy reports included: the reads the router is there to keep off your database, at the moment there are most of them. It is now refused with `53000` (insufficient resources) and a message that says why and what to do, for example `ERROR: the copy is busy (copy_wait_timeout): it waited as long as a statement may for a worker of the copy, and none came free. The statement was not run, and was not sent to your PostgreSQL either, ...`. This is what the MySQL router does (error 1040 there). **What a client sees:** an error where it used to get an answer from the source. The connection stays usable, and a prepared statement is tried on the copy again at its next execution. A client or pool that retries class 53 errors gets its answer once a worker is free. **How to avoid it:** give the copy more workers (`PG_ROUTER_WORKERS` in the compose `.env`, `--workers`) or a longer `--max-wait`. With no `--max-wait`, the default, a statement waits for its turn as long as its client stays connected, so this error comes in practice only where `--max-wait` is set. The refusals are counted as `refused` under `copy_wait_timeout` or `copy_queue_full` in `SHOW dbtrail.router_status`, `/status` and the status line. The same holds when the copy is too busy to list its columns, which the router reads every 10 seconds per connection: the source's plan still decides, and only what it would send to the copy is refused. Unchanged: a statement the source's plan finds cheap, one the router sends to the source for what it holds (`now()`, text sorted by collation and the rest), a transaction block, and a server without a forwarding user (which answered a busy copy with `53000` before too).
