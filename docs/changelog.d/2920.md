- **Engine Routing answers `AVG` of a whole-number column from the copy, with MySQL's decimals** (#2920). Since #2806 every `AVG` went to MySQL under routing, because the copy's own average is a double (`2.3333333333333335` where MySQL answers `2.3333`), and before that a routed `AVG` could come back without MySQL's four decimals (`119` where MariaDB answers `119.0000`). Now `AVG(k)` and `AVG(DISTINCT k)` over a `TINYINT` to `BIGINT` column (signed or not, `BOOL` included) are sent to the copy as a function that averages with whole numbers and rounds a half away from zero, as MySQL does, so the text, the column type (`DECIMAL`) and its four decimals are MySQL's, also near the limits of `BIGINT` and `BIGINT UNSIGNED`. Measured on MySQL 8.0 and MariaDB 10.11. The `AVG` has to be a whole column of the select list: MySQL computes it on more digits than it prints, so an `AVG` in arithmetic, a comparison, another function, `HAVING`, `ORDER BY` (by its alias or its position too) or a window still goes to MySQL, and so does an `AVG` over a `DECIMAL`, a `FLOAT` or `DOUBLE`, text, a date or an expression, the issue's `AVG(LENGTH(c))` among them. The port without routing and the console still answer `AVG` as a double, as documented.
