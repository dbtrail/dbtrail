- **PostgreSQL port: connections that have not logged in no longer keep clients out, and each username has its own SCRAM salt** (#3097).
  - **A full port makes room for a newcomer.** A connection holds one of the port's places (`--max-connections`, 256 by default) from the moment it is accepted, and one that never logged in held it for up to 30 seconds. When every place is taken and a new connection arrives, the port now closes the connection that has held its place longest without sending its proof of the password, if it has held it for more than 1 second, and gives its place to the newcomer; one that sent nothing goes before one that started its login. A connection whose proof reached the port keeps its place while it is checked and its server is opened, and a client that has logged in is never closed to make room: with every place a client's, the newcomer gets `53300`, as before.
  - **A salt for each username.** The port sent every client the same SCRAM salt, made up when the process started. The salt is now worked out from the username and a secret of the process: the same for a username until the process restarts, made the same way for a username nobody has, shared by no two usernames. Nothing changes for a client. The first login under a username after a start, or after one of the port's passwords changed, takes a few tens of milliseconds more, the same whether the username exists or not. The keys the port keeps are bounded (1,024 usernames a login succeeded under, and 1,024 of all the others, kept apart), it derives keys for at most 4 logins at once with 64 waiting, and a login past that is answered `53300` ("the port is checking as many logins as it takes at once; try again in a moment") instead of waiting. A connection that ended derives nothing.
  - `docs/postgres.md` now says how long a connection has to log in (30 seconds) and how large a message may be before the login (64 KB) and after it (16 MB).

  **Upgrading:** a client that takes more than 1 second between connecting and sending its password can be closed when the port is full, and connects again. A client or a pool that reads `53300` as "try again" needs no change.
