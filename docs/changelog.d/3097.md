- **PostgreSQL port: connections that have not logged in no longer keep clients out of a full port** (#3097). A connection holds one of the port's places (`--max-connections`, 256 by default) from the moment it is accepted, and one that never logged in held it for up to 30 seconds. When every place is taken and a new connection arrives, the port now closes the connection that has held its place longest without sending its proof of the password, if it has held it for more than 1 second, and gives its place to the newcomer; one that sent nothing goes before one that started its login. A connection whose proof reached the port keeps its place while it is checked and its server is opened, and a client that has logged in is never closed to make room: with every place a client's, the newcomer gets `53300`, as before. This protects the port from connections that sit idle. It does not protect it from a client that connects again as fast as it is closed, and it is not a limit per address: the port still belongs behind a firewall or a proxy. `docs/postgres.md` now also says how long a connection has to log in (30 seconds) and how large a message may be before the login (64 KB) and after it (16 MB).

  **Upgrading:** a client that takes more than 1 second between connecting and sending its password can be closed when the port is full, and connects again. How passwords are checked did not change.
