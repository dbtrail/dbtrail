- **PostgreSQL port: connections that have not logged in no longer keep clients out, and each username has its own SCRAM salt** (#3097).
  - **A full port makes room for a newcomer.** A connection holds one of the port's places (`--max-connections`, 256 by default) from the moment it is accepted, and one that never logged in held it for up to 30 seconds. When every place is taken and a new connection arrives, the port now closes the connection that has held its place longest without sending its proof of the password, if it has held it for more than 1 second, and gives its place to the newcomer; one that sent nothing goes before one that started its login. A connection whose proof reached the port keeps its place while it is checked and its server is opened, and a client that has logged in is never closed to make room: with every place a client's, the newcomer gets `53300`, as before.
  - **A salt for each username.** The port sent every client the same SCRAM salt, made up when the process started. The salt is now worked out from the username and a secret of the process: the same for a username until the process restarts, made the same way for a username nobody has, shared by no two usernames. Any username opens the port with the port's password, so a username's keys are derived the first time someone logs in under it after a start or a change of the port's passwords, and kept. A login that fails is never answered sooner than 250 ms after its proof reached the port, whether the keys were in memory or not, so the time a refusal takes does not say whether a username is in use; a login that succeeds is not held back. Logins under one username that arrive together derive its keys once, so a pool that reconnects after a restart is not turned away. The port derives for at most half the machine's cores (1 to 4) at once with 64 logins waiting; a login past that, and any login that fails while the line is that full, is answered `53300` ("the port is checking as many logins as it takes at once; try again in a moment"). The keys kept are bounded: 1,024 usernames a login succeeded under and 1,024 of all the others, kept apart.
  - `docs/postgres.md` now says how long a connection has to log in (30 seconds) and how large a message may be before the login (64 KB) and after it (16 MB).

  **Upgrading:** a wrong password is now answered after 250 ms, not at once. A client that takes more than 1 second between connecting and sending its password can be closed when the port is full, and connects again. A client or a pool that reads `53300` as "try again" needs no change.
