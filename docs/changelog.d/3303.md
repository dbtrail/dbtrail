- **The PostgreSQL port learns the order written from a slow statement too** (#3303). A statement that answers on the copy in the engine's own join order after 15 seconds or more of engine time marks its shape, and the next statement of that shape is tried with its joins in the order written, with the join check of that order and at most half of what is left of `statement_timeout` where the source can take the rest. If the trial answers in at most half the slow run's time, the shape is remembered exactly like one learned from running out of memory (24 hours, same forgetting). If it is not that much faster, the engine's own order is kept for that shape for 24 hours. If it fails in the order written (out of memory, out of time, `join_too_big`), it is not sent to the source: it runs once more in the engine's own order, and that order is kept for 24 hours. Measured on the CH-benCHmark's Q7 over fresh-mode views, the engine's own order flips between about 7 s and 31 to 33 s, while the order written answers in 5.7 to 6.6 s. `/status` counts `written_order_slow_marked`, `written_order_trial`, `written_order_learned_slow` and `written_order_own_order_kept` under `join_checks`, and these no longer count as `written_order_remembered`.
